# Compare like with like

Establish the question: replacement, complement, performance comparison or shared
risk. Do not assume the first upload is the benchmark or invent an overall winner.
Use explicit comparison roles required by the tool. Let the MCP resolve a default
benchmark only for calculations that require one; explain its financial role from
the result. Do not add a market reference to every comparison.

Use the preparation layer's supported common intervals, currency/basis conventions
and coverage. Disclose material changes and distinguish overlapping-period results
from each investment's full history. Do not splice periods, silently translate
currencies or assert identical fitted samples when results differ.

Before interpreting differences in factor results, check the returned selected
factors, model specification and estimation samples. A shared candidate catalogue
does not mean the fitted models are identical. Explain material differences and
qualify the comparison; request a common specification only when the tools support
it. Never refit informally or repeatedly change settings to obtain a preferred story.

Begin with the smallest comparison that answers the question. Performance/risk
and drawdown may suffice initially. Correlations, factor attribution or rolling
views should resolve a specific uncertainty, not fill a fixed report template.
Retain every requested investment or explain missing results; do not rank from
partial pages or interpret unavailable correlations as zero.

For replacement, discuss similarities and trade-offs supported by the evidence.
For complement, discuss common drivers and observed diversification, without
claiming that historical relationships guarantee future protection. State any
user criteria separately from your interpretations. Do not invent scores,
allocation recommendations or optimization output.
